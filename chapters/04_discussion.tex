\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Summary of Findings}

This section interprets the results reported in Section 4 against the theoretical framework of Section 2 and assesses the three research questions and the five hypotheses. This study rebuilt the country-specific structural VAR of Beirne and Sugandi (2023) for Japan, estimated it over the paper period with 2021-era data vintages, and extended it through June 2026. The replication reproduces the paper's core findings for Japan at the daily frequency, namely the negative and statistically significant real GDP response, the sharp and persistent REER appreciation, the positive and persistent bond spread widening, and the null responses of the portfolio flow variables. Nine of the seventeen variable-frequency comparisons against the published figures are matches, five are partial matches, and three are mismatches. The mismatches are confined to the World Uncertainty Index at both frequencies and to other investment at the daily frequency. The extension reveals that the real channel attenuates, with the monthly RGDP response statistically indistinguishable from zero in the extended sample, while the price channels survive in weakened form. The direct investment channel produces the only statistically significant capital flow response in the analysis, a finding the paper does not report.

\subsection{Interpretation and Connection to the Literature}

The findings summarized above line up with the theoretical framework developed in Section 2. The REER appreciation and the spread widening are the flight-to-safety channel operating through prices, and they are the most robust findings of the replication, matching the paper's central narrative that risk-off shocks drive investors into yen-denominated assets \parencite{beirne_sugandi_2023}. The null capital flow responses are consistent with the offshore derivative and repatriation mechanisms of \textcite{botman_carvalho_lam_2013} and the portfolio rebalancing channel of \textcite{hau_rey_2006}, which separate the price response from the measured volume response. The negative RGDP response is the competitiveness channel of \textcite{masujima_2017} and \textcite{belke_volz_2020}, with the exchange rate appreciation transmitting into output with the lag that \textcite{iwaisako_nakata_2017} document.

The WUI mismatch deserves a careful reading. The paper finds no statistically significant uncertainty response for Japan, and the replication also fails to find a significant response, since the daily trough band and the monthly peak band both span zero. The two studies therefore agree on the significance verdict of Hypothesis 5, and the mismatch is confined to the sign and shape of an insignificant point estimate. The sign instability of the WUI response across frequencies, in both the paper and the replication, reflects the episode dependence of Japan's uncertainty dynamics, where global episodes raise the index and foreign-specific episodes lower it because Japan reads as the safe haven, a fragility amplified by the quarterly-to-daily interpolation of the COVID spike-crash sequence.

The extension result connects directly to the post-2021 literature added in Section 2. The attenuation of the RGDP response in the extended sample is consistent with \textcite{park_fang_2025}, who find that the yen's crisis appreciation has become conditional on the US-Japan rate differential and the type of shock, and with the carry trade unwind of August 2024 documented by \textcite{aquilina_lombardi_schrimpf_sushko_2024}, in which the yen appreciated through deleveraging mechanics rather than safe haven inflows. The extension period contains the Omicron episode, the Russia-Ukraine episode, the August 2024 unwind, the 2025 tariff episodes, and the 2026 Middle East escalation, and the monthly evidence says that a risk-off shock no longer produces the output decline that the paper period delivered. The price channels still respond, but the real spillover that made the Japanese case distinctive has weakened.

The event record of the extension period sharpens the interpretation. The April 2025 tariff shock produced the clearest counterexample to the depreciation narrative, with the yen strengthening toward 140 as the dollar failed to behave as a safe haven, and the episode registers in the risk-off table as the March to April 2025 tariff bands. The sample closes in June 2026 with the Bank of Japan's policy rate at 1.00 percent and the yen again near 160, which confirms that the rate differential with the United States, rather than the safe-haven mechanism, set the currency's trend over the extension. The conditional reading is corroborated by the surveillance and academic evidence cited in Section 2. The International Monetary Fund attributes the yen's weakness to interest rate differentials and documents exchange rate volatility beyond what rate expectations alone explain \parencite{imf_2025}, and \textcite{lu_yu_li_li_2024} show that no East Asian currency, including the yen, qualifies as a safe haven when uncertainty is measured by geopolitical risk or trade policy uncertainty, while the yen retains haven properties under VIX-based risk. The sign instability of our WUI response across episode types is the VAR-level manifestation of exactly this conditionality, global episodes raise Japanese uncertainty and foreign-specific episodes lower it because Japan reads as the safe haven.

The direct investment finding adds a nuance to the paper's blanket capital flow null. Direct investment reflects long-horizon commitments, and the theory would predict it to be the flow category most insulated from short-term risk-off dynamics. The replication finds a statistically significant positive response at the three-month horizon in both the restricted and unrestricted monthly specifications, the only significant flow response among the forty flow-horizon entries tested. The result should be read cautiously, given the source-data differences and the single-horizon significance, but it suggests that risk-off shocks may affect long-horizon investment decisions even when portfolio flows show no consistent pattern.

\subsection{Answering the Research Questions}

The three research questions posed in the introduction are answered in turn. The first research question asked whether the paper's qualitative structure for Japan reproduces when the model is re-estimated independently on the original sample. The answer is yes for the channels the paper's thesis hinges on. The negative RGDP response, the REER appreciation, the spread widening, and the null portfolio flows all reproduce at the daily frequency, and the monthly frequency confirms the signs. The WUI and other investment responses do not reproduce, and each mismatch has a documented mechanism.

The second research question asked whether the estimated relationships survive into the post-2021 regime. The answer is partial. The price channels survive, with the spread response remaining positive and significant and the daily REER response remaining positive in the extended sample. The real channel does not at the monthly frequency. The monthly extended RGDP response is an order of magnitude smaller than the paper period and statistically indistinguishable from zero at every horizon, while the daily extended response remains negative and significant, so the attenuation is frequency-dependent and consistent with the weakened safe-haven role of the yen during the rate divergence cycle.

The third research question asked which results are robust to data revisions and which depend on the data vintage. The RGDP response is the vintage-dependent result, since the restricted model's sign flips positive on current-vintage GDP and negative on the ALFRED 2021-06-01 vintage. The REER and spread responses are robust to the vintage choice in direction, and the capital flow responses are robust in their nullity. The implication is that a researcher downloading the data today and running the paper's specification as published would obtain the opposite output sign, which makes the vintage policy a substantive choice rather than a documentation nicety.

\subsection{Hypotheses Assessment}

The five hypotheses from Section 2 are assessed against the evidence from both tiers and both samples.

Hypothesis 1 concerned the exchange rate channel. The null of no significant REER response is rejected. The daily and monthly responses are positive and significant in the paper period, and the daily response remains positive and significant in the extension.

Hypothesis 2 concerned the output channel. The null of no significant RGDP response is rejected for the paper period, where the daily and monthly responses are negative with bands below zero. The null is not rejected in the extended sample at the monthly frequency, where the response is indistinguishable from zero, while the daily tier retains a significant negative response, so the verdict is period- and frequency-dependent.

Hypothesis 3 concerned the bond market channel. The null of no significant spread response is rejected. The spread response is positive and significant in both frequencies and both samples.

Hypothesis 4 concerned the capital flow channel. The null of no significant portfolio flow response is not rejected, matching the paper's null result and the theoretical prediction of the offshore derivative channel. The exception is direct investment, which shows a significant positive response at the three-month horizon in the monthly specification, so the capital flow null is upheld for the portfolio categories and qualified for direct investment.

Hypothesis 5 concerned the uncertainty channel. The null of no significant WUI response is not rejected, matching the paper's conclusion, although the point estimates carry the opposite sign in the replication and the cross-validation of the response shape fails at both frequencies.

\subsection{Limitations}

Eight limitations bound the interpretation above, each cross-referenced to the analysis that it constrains. First, the paper does not report the EViews interpolation variant, the lag maximum searched, or the confidence interval method, so every replication of a recursively identified VAR from a paper with unreported software settings carries irreducible uncertainty about whether differences originate in data, code, or configuration. Second, the RGDP response is fragile to the GDP vintage, and on current-vintage data the sign reverses, which is the single most consequential limitation for the replication claim. Third, the restricted and unrestricted specifications diverge on current-vintage data, and the divergence signals model sensitivity to GDP revisions that cannot be resolved without the paper's original data and software environment. Fourth, the capital flow series come from the Bank of Japan's BPM6 statistics rather than the paper's IMF IFS source, and although the two are directly comparable under BPM6 conventions, the level offset in the debt flow response and the phase mismatch in the other investment response are consistent with a source-data effect. Fifth, the WUI is available at quarterly frequency for the full sample, while the paper states a monthly series that cannot exist before 2008, and every replication of the 1999 sample must use the quarterly series. Sixth, the BIS REER vintage rests on a single Wayback Machine snapshot from May 2021, because the BIS does not publicly archive retrospective vintages. Seventh, the AIC overfits to the boundary lag on this data, and the monthly tier uses the BIC instead, a divergence from the paper's stated criterion that is documented but cannot be compared against the paper's unpublished diagnostics. Eighth, the Monte Carlo confidence bands differ between x86 and ARM architectures at machine precision, with differences in the sixth decimal place that are negligible for the match classifications but would prevent bit-for-bit reproduction across hardware.

\subsection{Implications}

Within the limits just stated, the findings carry implications for policy and for replication practice. For policymakers, the attenuation of the output channel in the extended sample is not evidence that the safe-haven problem has disappeared. It is evidence that the exchange rate channel has become conditional on the monetary policy constellation, in the direction that \textcite{park_fang_2025} describe, and the August 2024 episode shows that the carry trade unwind channel can still move the yen sharply when risk appetite turns \parencite{aquilina_lombardi_schrimpf_sushko_2024}. The persistence of the spread response in the extension, with the Bank of Japan normalizing policy, suggests that sovereign bond market channels remain live for financial stability monitoring.

For replication practice, the study demonstrates that data vintage is a first-order identification choice in a replication of this kind. The five code defects corrected during the replication, and the sign reversal induced by current-vintage GDP, are the kind of finding that a replication report exists to disclose. The honest boundary of the exercise is the three mismatches, each with a documented mechanism, and the reader is left to judge whether the paper's qualitative conclusions for Japan survive the independent rebuild. On the evidence here, the answer is yes for the price channels and the output channel over the paper period, and only partially for the output channel over the extended sample, which weakens at the monthly frequency. Section 6 concludes the paper by drawing these findings together, stating the boundaries of the replication claim, and looking ahead to future research.
